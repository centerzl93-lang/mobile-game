# PLAYTEST.md — known issues & status

Known playtest issues, test flakiness, and open balance questions, with their current status. Every
item here is real and sourced from the code, the test suite, or `HANDOFF.md` — nothing speculative.
Balance items marked **needs-playtest** are deliberately *not* decided in code; they want real
playthroughs (or a player call), not a guess.

Status: 🔴 open · 🟡 mitigated / watching · 🟢 by-design (working as intended, flagged to watch) ·
⚪ won't-fix (documented workaround) · 🔵 decision needed.

| B7 | **`NO_TOOLS_PENALTY` (0.6) was acting as a major production multiplier, not a modest one — retuned to 0.75.** Playtest report: with tools stocked, food needed ~50% of the workforce (the design's own anchor); once the barns ran dry, food needed ~75%, leaving too few hands for wood/firewood and killing the run before winter — a *temporary* tool shortage produced a near-unrecoverable cascade. The audit: no tool (0.6) vs iron (1.0) vs steel (1.15) is a 1.67× swing between "no tools" and "iron", applied to every non-farm trade (`workOutput`'s `tf` multiplies gatherer/fishing/hunting/lumberyard/quarry/mine/woodcutter/blacksmith/luxury/tailor output *and* `labour()`'s builder-work — farms alone are exempt, they answer to no chisel). A 1/0.6 ≈ 1.67× workforce cost to stand still (50% → 83%) is what a genuinely "modest" bonus should never do; a 1/0.75 ≈ 1.33× cost (50% → 66.7%) is a real bite that still leaves a third of the workforce for everything else. **Change:** `NO_TOOLS_PENALTY` 0.6 → 0.75 (matches `COLD_WORK_FACTOR`, this codebase's existing "going without, but still working" tier, rather than `COLD_WORK_MIN`'s crisis-grade 0.6). `IRON_TOOL_PROD` (1.0, the tuning anchor) and `STEEL_TOOL_PROD` (1.15 — already a modest, non-"overwhelming" edge, and the real reason to bother with steel is `STEEL_DURABILITY`'s 2×, unchanged) are untouched — the swing that needed narrowing was the bottom of the ladder, not the top. Difficulty's starting tool stockpiles (48 Normal / 24 Hard) are untouched too: the audit found the *penalty magnitude*, not the *starting quantity*, was the lever making a temporary shortage catastrophic, and the gentler penalty alone makes Hard's leaner 24-tool opening survivable without also making it a bigger handout. Guarded by new tests: the exact tier factors (`tier factor` test, updated for 0.75), a Normal village surviving three tool-less seasons without deaths, and Hard clearing its opening seasons on its own starting kit. | 🟢 by-design | The one thing this change *cannot* touch: once a village is self-sufficient in tools, Hard and Normal run identical production/consumption math (see B4) — difficulty is only ever the opening stockpile. A fully-tooled Hard is still strictly harder than Normal (half the starting food/fuel runway, tested), but it is no harder *by design* than that gap once both villages are self-sufficient; making it so would be a difficulty-system change, not a tool-balance one, and is out of this audit's scope. Watch in play whether the new 0.75/1.0/1.15 ladder still feels like it matters — if losing tools reads as too soft now, ease back toward 0.7; if a temporary shortage still spirals, push toward 0.8. |
| B8 | **Tools moved from a village-wide switch to a per-villager belonging**, on top of B7's value retune. The old model (`villageToolTier`) read *any* tool anywhere in the barns and lifted every worker in the village to that tier at once — the instant the last tool anywhere broke, every trade in the village dropped to `NO_TOOLS_PENALTY` simultaneously, a cliff-edge that made a single unlucky wear-out event look like a village-wide crisis even with, say, eleven of twelve workers still perfectly equipped. **Change:** each citizen now carries their own `tool` (`'iron' \| 'steel' \| undefined`) and `toolWear`. A bare-handed villager equips the next time they are naturally standing at a barn for another reason — dropping off a load, fetching a converter input, fetching or returning builder materials (`tryEquipTool`, steel first) — nobody is sent on a dedicated trip. Wear (`wearCitizenTool`) accrues onto *that villager's own* tool as they work, at the same per-cycle / per-builder-work rates as before, and clears it when it breaks. `citizenToolFactor(c)` replaces `toolProdFactor(s)` at every call site (`workOutput`, `labour`). Effect: a tool shortage is now a *gradient* across the workforce — some villagers equipped, some between barn visits — rather than a single switch, so the village's overall productivity degrades in proportion to how many tools are actually short, not in one lump the moment the barns hit zero. `workplaceStatus`'s "slowed" line and the villager sheet's Tool row both now read per-worker/per-citizen instead of the old village-wide fact. | 🟢 by-design | Watch two things in play: (1) whether the gradual, per-worker degradation reads as clearly to the player as the old all-at-once switch did — the workplace status line ("N/M bare-handed") is the intended replacement cue; (2) whether opportunistic-only pickup (no dedicated fetch trip) leaves some job types (ones that rarely visit a barn) staying bare-handed for a long stretch even when the barns are well stocked — if that shows up as a real problem, the fix is a dedicated low-priority "go get a tool" errand for a long-bare-handed villager, not reverting to the village-wide model. |
| B9 | **Steel tools carried their own stockpile-limit row, duplicating the plain-tool one.** The HUD chip and the villager's own kit (B8) already treat iron and steel as one "have we got tools" figure, but the stockpile-limits panel and `limitedOutput` still gave a steel-recipe smith its own `'steeltools'` cap, independent of the iron one — so a player could cap "Tools" at 50 expecting to slow the whole tool supply and find a steel smith still running flat out because its *separate* cap was still empty. **Change:** `limitedOutput` now answers `'tools'` for a blacksmith on either recipe, and `limitStock(s, 'tools')` reads the *combined* iron+steel total (mirroring `totalFood`'s pattern for its own category) rather than each kind separately; `steeltools` is dropped from `LIMITABLE` so the panel offers one "Tools" row instead of two. `s.limits.steeltools` on an old save is simply never read again — harmless leftover data, no migration needed. Guarded by a new test ("one tools cap covers iron and steel together, not one seam at a time") that caps `tools` at 50, fills it with plain iron alone, and confirms a *steel-recipe* smith still reads as at-limit. | 🟢 by-design | None outstanding — this folds the limit system into the same one-cap-for-both model the HUD and the tool ladder already used, rather than introducing a new concept. |
| B11 | **Resource-conversion & tool-efficiency audit** (iron/steel tools, Regular/Warm clothing, the luxury chain, mine/quarry/woodcutter outputs). Two findings, one fixed in code, one decided and left alone. **(1) Dead data, fixed.** `types.ts` carried a second, unused "per season" copy of every converter recipe (`SMITH_IRON_TOOLS_OUT` 8, `TAILOR_CLOTHING_OUT` 6, …) that nothing read — the numbers `workOutput`/`converterInputs` actually ran on (`SMITH_IRON_OUT` 5/cycle, `TAILOR_OUT` 4/cycle, …) lived as private consts in `simulation.ts` and had drifted out of sync with those doc comments, in defiance of this file's own "balance changes are one-line edits to `types.ts`" convention. **Change:** the live converter-recipe constants (`LOAD_FOOD`/`LOAD_MAT`, `WCUT_*`, `MINE_*_FACTOR`, `SMITH_*`, `TAILOR_*`, `LUX_*`) moved to `types.ts`, exported, and `simulation.ts` now imports them instead of shadowing them locally — no recipe numbers changed, only where they live. Terrain-richness factors (`FOREST_CIRCLE_IDEAL`, `QUARRY_ROCK_BONUS`, …) stayed in `simulation.ts`, since they scale a site's own yield rather than convert one resource into another. **(2) Tools stay a yield multiplier, not a speed one — audited, not changed.** The brief asked whether `citizenToolFactor` (0.75/1.00/1.15, see B7) should instead shorten `WORK_SECONDS` per tool tier. It should not, for three reasons found in the code itself: *(a)* `WORK_SECONDS` is one constant shared by every converter and gatherer/hunter/quarry/mine job — making it tool-dependent would touch every one of them, not just the blacksmith/tailor this brief named. *(b)* Tool wear is deliberately charged **per completed cycle**, calibrated so cycle cadence — not tool tier — decides how often a tool wears (see `TOOL_WEAR_PER_CYCLE`'s own doc comment: "an educated or steel-equipped worker … wears at the same pace"); a steel-tooled worker finishing cycles faster would silently wear through **more** tools per season than a bare-handed one, undercutting `STEEL_DURABILITY`'s whole "lasts twice as long" pitch. *(c)* The two models are mathematically the same total output over any window long enough for cycles to average out — 15% more per cycle and 15% fewer seconds per cycle both raise seasonal throughput by 15%. Switching mechanisms doesn't unlock new design space; it only risks the wear-cadence coupling in (b) across a heavily-playtested economy (B6/B7/B8) for no balance gain. Confirmed by a new sim test forking one bootstrapped village three ways (bare/iron/steel): iron consumed per fixed time window is the same across all three tools (cycle cadence is tool-independent) while tools produced per iron consumed tracks `citizenToolFactor` exactly. **Builders already use the speed model** (`labour()` scales builder-work laid *per second* by `citizenToolFactor`, not a discrete per-cycle output) — appropriately, since a build site's `work` is a continuous quantity with no per-cycle wear boundary to protect, unlike a producer's. The split is intentional, not an inconsistency to fix. **Ratios spot-checked and left alone:** the leather-vs-wool clothing asymmetry (`TAILOR_LEATHER_IN` 5 vs `TAILOR_WOOL_IN` 4 for the same `TAILOR_OUT` 4) looks unfair to leather until weighed against supply — wool is a sheep pen's entire dedicated yield (up to `LOAD_MAT` a cycle), leather is a hunter's incidental byproduct (`HUNT_HIDE_FRACTION` 0.4 of `LOAD_MAT`, off a cabin built for food) — so the recipe's own comment ("a pen of sheep is the real difference") holds: wool trades a dedicated building for efficiency, leather trades efficiency for zero marginal infrastructure. Steel's recipe (`SMITH_STEEL_IRON`/`COAL` 4/3 → `SMITH_STEEL_OUT` 5, same *count* as plain iron) confirmed to add only coal, never more iron, for the same tool count — durability and the 15% carry the tier, not a bigger anvil. The luxury chain's value-multiplier compresses climbing the chain (sand→glass ×2, glass→jewelry ×1.43, →fine jewellery ×1.33) rather than holding constant — expected, since sand is "worth barely carrying" while gold/dye/silk are real imported cost, and every step still clears a real margin. | 🟢 by-design | New coverage: `sim-tests/production-ratios.test.ts` (exact recipe quantities via `debugConverterInputs`; the yield-not-speed A/B/C fork; `citizenToolFactor`'s exact ladder; `STEEL_DURABILITY`'s 2× cycle count; idle-worker wear is zero; a season-long run never leaves a store negative/non-finite; the steel/warm-clothing cost-asymmetry assertions). No recipe numbers, trade values, or tool constants changed — this was a data-location and documentation pass plus a design decision recorded, not a rebalance. |
